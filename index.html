Fire Safety · Older Buildings

The Queue

More than ten thousand of Hong Kong's older buildings need fire-safety work. Public capacity covers a few dozen a year. So the real question is not whether the buildings are dangerous — it is who goes first.

An explainer for residents · Sources: Legislative Council papers, 2024
New Lucky House, Jordan · Photo: FONO_25_RUOQWM_SAXM via Wikimedia Commons (CC0)
01The Case

A building everyone knew about

In April 2024, a No. 3 alarm fire broke out at New Lucky House in Jordan. Five people died and forty were injured. The building was sixty years old, subdivided into flats and guesthouses — home to some of the city's most vulnerable residents.

Fire Safety Directions had been issued to the building sixteen years earlier. They were never complied with. When the fire came, the alarm system, the hose reels and the fire extinguishers all failed to work.

The problem was not that nobody knew it was dangerous. It never reached the front of the queue.

The queue is not an abstraction. According to the 2021 Population Census, 87.9% of households in subdivided flats live in buildings aged 50 years or above — the people least able to escape a fire are the most likely to be living in a building that is still waiting.

03The Method

How do you decide who goes first?

When the pool is ten thousand buildings and the capacity is a few dozen, the order cannot be left to chance or to whoever complains loudest. During the amendment's scrutiny, the Government itself undertook to set up a scoring system to set priorities.

  1. Collect public dataBuilding age and use, inspection-notice compliance, unauthorised-works orders, the age profile of the neighbourhood.
  2. Score the riskCombine the factors into a score. A high score does not mean "this building will burn" — it means "this building is worth checking first".
  3. Produce a priority listThe list informs the order of works-in-default, inspections and the installation of fire-detection devices.
  • 2007The Fire Safety (Buildings) Ordinance requires pre-1987 buildings to upgrade to modern standards.
  • 2024An amendment empowers the Government to carry out works in default of owners and recover the costs — and to set priorities for doing so.
  • 2025The Fire Services Department pilots IoT fire detectors in 10 old buildings, with a possible roll-out to 3,600 — another queue that needs an order.

A note on limits: ranking tools are internal aids for professionals. Individual building scores should not be published — what belongs in public is policy, resources and self-help knowledge.

04What You Can Do

Four things within your power

  • Keep escape routes clear. Uncluttered corridors, staircases and exits are the cheapest and most effective fire protection there is.
  • Read your building's notices. If your building has received Fire Safety Directions, the owners' corporation is legally required to act — owners are entitled to ask about progress.
  • Report blocked or altered escape routes. Contact the Fire Services Department or 1823, especially in subdivided buildings.
  • Check on elderly residents. Older people in ageing districts are the least able to escape. One visit, one check, can change the outcome.
A five-minute check you can do tonight
You do not need any equipment — just walk the route you would take in a fire.
  1. Walk from your flat to the street. Are the corridor, staircase and exit clear of clutter?
  2. Do the smoke-lobby and staircase doors close by themselves — and is none of them wedged open or locked?
  3. Are the exit signs lit and pointing the way you actually walked?
  4. Is the hose reel and extinguisher cabinet intact, accessible and not used for storage?
  5. If anything fails this list, tell your owners' corporation or management office — or report it to 1823.
Check your own building — three official entry points
  • BRAVO · Buildings Department — look up your building's records, approved plans and statutory orders online.
  • FSD Building Improvement Support Centre — one-stop help for owners and occupiers who have received Fire Safety Directions. Tel 2272 9112 · Mon–Sat 9:00–20:00
  • 1823 — report blocked or altered escape routes and damaged fire-service installations.
  • Fire Safety Improvement Works Subsidy Scheme — the Government subsidises eligible owners for the required works; ask the Support Centre or call the URA Building Rehabilitation hotline. Tel 3188 1188
